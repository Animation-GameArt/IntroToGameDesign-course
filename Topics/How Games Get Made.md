# How Games Get Made

**Week 1 · [[Lecture] Week 1](https://github.com/Animation-GameArt/IntroToGameDesign-course/blob/main/Class%20Pages/Week%2001%20-%20Lecture.md)**

> [!abstract] In one sentence
> Games are made in stages, from an idea to a release, and this course follows the same stages on a small scale.

## What this topic is for

Students should see the semester as a production with a shape, not as a list of assignments. After this topic they should be able to name the roles on a game team, the stages of a production, and which weeks of the course match which stage.

## Key ideas

- **Roles.** Design decides what the game is and how it plays. Art and audio decide how it looks and sounds. Programming makes it work. Production keeps it on schedule. Quality assurance (QA) finds what's broken.
- **Indie and AAA.** A AAA game may have hundreds of people in each role. An indie game may have one person in all of them. Students in this course are solo developers, which is the strongest argument for a small scope.
- **The stages of a production:**

| Stage | What happens | In this course |
|---|---|---|
| Concept | Ideas, pitch, approval | Weeks 1–3 |
| Pre-production | Design documents, prototypes | Weeks 3–7 |
| Production | Building the game | Weeks 8–12 |
| Alpha | Every feature exists | Week 12 |
| Beta | All content is in | Week 14 |
| Gold | Released | Week 15 |
| Post-mortem | Looking back | Week 16 |

- **Design documents are tools, not paperwork.** The one-sheet gets an idea approved. The ten-pager turns it into a plan. The backlog turns the plan into tasks.

## In class

1. Put the stage table on the screen next to the course schedule.
2. Show the credits of a well-known game to make the scale of a AAA team concrete, then the credits of a solo indie game.
