# Watching Someone Play

**Week 9 · [[Lecture] Week 9](https://github.com/Animation-GameArt/IntroToGameDesign-course/blob/main/Class%20Pages/Week%2009%20-%20Lecture.md)**

> [!abstract] In one sentence
> The fastest way to find what's wrong with a game is to hand it to someone else and say nothing.

## What this topic is for

A two-minute version of the Week 13 playtest, practiced in lab. Students learn the one rule early: don't explain.

## Key ideas

- **The developer can't see their own game fresh.** They know every control and every path.
- **Say nothing.** No hints, no apologies, no "that's a bug." The player's confusion is the information.
- **Watch, don't ask.** Where do they hesitate? What do they try that doesn't work? Where do they lean in?
- **Write it down.** Memory keeps the flattering parts.
- **Two minutes is enough** to learn whether the first screen teaches the game.

## In class

1. Pair students. Each plays the other's build for two minutes in silence, then swaps.
2. Each developer writes down three things they saw.
