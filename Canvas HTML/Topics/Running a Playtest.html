Week 13 · [Lecture] Week 13

In one sentence
Three people play your game while you watch silently and take notes, and what they do tells you more than anything they say.

What this topic is for

Ex 10 is a structured playtest. Students learn to run a session, record it, and turn it into three prioritized changes.

Key ideas

  • One sentence to start: "Play this, and say what you're thinking." Then stay silent.
  • No help. No hints, no apologies, no "that's a bug." If the player is completely stuck for a long time, note it, then move them on.
  • Take notes in three columns:
Key ideas
Hesitated or got stuck Tried something that didn't work Smiled, leaned in, or said "oh!"
  • Doing beats saying. Players are polite and poor at explaining themselves. "It was fun" from someone who quit after two minutes means less than the quitting.
  • Patterns matter. One player stuck is an anecdote. Three stuck at the same place is the problem.
  • Read difficulty from failures. Note where each player failed and how many tries a section took. Compare with the difficulty the ten-pager intended.
  • Ask your prepared questions after, not during.
  • Choose three changes that affected the most players, and put them at the top of the board.

In class

  1. Run one session in front of the class with a volunteer, modeling the silence.
  2. Students run three sessions, about ten minutes each.
