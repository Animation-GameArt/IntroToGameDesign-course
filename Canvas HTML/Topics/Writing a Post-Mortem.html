Week 16 · [Lecture] Week 16

In one sentence
A post-mortem is an honest review of a finished project, written so the next project goes better.

What this topic is for

Students present a five-minute post-mortem in Week 16. It's graded on honesty and insight, not on how well the project went.

Key ideas

  • What went right, so it can be repeated.
  • What went wrong, so it can be avoided.
  • Specific, not general. "I ran out of time" is a symptom. "I spent three weeks on a save system the game didn't need" is a finding.
  • Plan against result. Put the ten-pager's scope page next to the finished game: what was cut, what was added, what changed, and why.
  • One lesson forward. One practice to use on the next project.
  • Not an apology, not an advertisement. Both are easy; neither is useful.

A structure for five minutes

A structure for five minutes
Minute Content
1 Show the game
2 Three things that went right
3 Three things that went wrong
4 Plan against result: the ten-pager's scope beside the finished game
5 What you'd do differently, and one lesson for next time
