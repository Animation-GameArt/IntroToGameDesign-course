Week 5 · [Lecture] Week 5

In one sentence
A good level introduces one idea safely, then asks the player to use it with something at stake, then combines it with something else.

What this topic is for

Students design levels by placing things until the space feels full. This topic gives them a method instead: sketch the level as a sequence of beats, each with a purpose, before placing a single tile. Ex 04's design decision asks for one teach beat and one test beat.

Key ideas

  • Teach. The player meets an idea where failing costs little: a small gap with a floor under it, one spike with plenty of room around it.
  • Test. The same idea, now with something at stake: the gap has a pit below; the spikes surround a narrow landing.
  • Twist. The idea combined with another: a gap crossed on a moving platform, spikes under a row of one-way platforms.
  • One new idea at a time. If a section introduces two things at once and the player fails, they can't tell which one beat them.
  • Pacing. Alternate tense stretches with calm ones. A checkpoint or a pickup after a hard section is a breath.
  • Guiding without words. A trail of coins shows where to jump. A lone enemy on flat ground teaches stomping before enemies appear on ledges.
  • Levels are written, then built. A line of beats on paper is quick to change. A painted Tilemap isn't.

In class

  1. Sketch a level as a row of boxes on the whiteboard, each labeled teach, test or twist and naming its idea.
  2. Play the first minute of a well-known platformer and label its beats together.
  3. Students sketch three beats for their own game.
