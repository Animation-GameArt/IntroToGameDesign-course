Week 1 · [Lecture] Week 1

In one sentence
A genre is a bundle of mechanics players already understand, and in this course choosing a genre from the map also chooses the scripts your game starts with.

What this topic is for

This lecture does two jobs. It gives students the vocabulary of genres, and it sets the course's main constraint: every student pitches a game that fits a genre on the Genre Map. Students should leave knowing which two or three genres interest them, and why the constraint helps them.

Key ideas

  • Genres describe what the player does, not what the game looks like or what it's about. A platformer about cooking is still a platformer.
  • Genres carry expectations. A player who starts a platformer expects to jump, and expects falling to cost something. Meeting expectations frees the designer to surprise players in one chosen place.
  • Most games mix genres. Choose the one that describes what the player does most of the time. That decides the kit.
  • The map is a scope tool. Each genre on it has a kit of scripts and a sample game, so the main mechanics already exist. Production time goes into the part of the game that is the student's own.
  • The map rates difficulty. "Good first game", "moderate" and "ambitious" describe how many systems must all work. Ambitious genres are allowed, with a small, clear scope.

In class

  1. Walk the summary table of the Genre Map. For each genre, name the core loop and one example everyone knows.
  2. Show one genre's kit sample being played, to make "the scripts already exist" concrete.
  3. Explain "Not on the map": VR, AR and very large genres, and the rule that ideas off the map are discussed before pitch week.

Common problems

Common problems
Students often… Help them by…
Pick a genre they love but that is rated ambitious Asking for the smallest version that would still be fun
Feel the map limits creativity Showing how much variety exists within one genre
Choose a 3D genre without 3D experience Pointing to "About 3D games" on the Genre Map

Where it comes up
