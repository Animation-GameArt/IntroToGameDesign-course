Week 8 · [Lecture] Week 8

In one sentence
A game should answer everything the player does, and adding that answer, called "juice," makes the same rules feel like a better game.

What this topic is for

This is the first week of students' own games. Before they add content, they learn that polishing the few actions the player repeats most has more effect than any new feature. Ex 07 asks for feedback on their three most common actions.

Key ideas

  • Every action gets an answer. A sound, a flash, a puff of dust, a shake, a number.
  • Layer it. Strong feedback usually combines several small things at once: sound, a visual effect, a little movement.
  • Size it to the event. A coin gets a small answer; defeating a boss gets a big one. If everything is loud, nothing stands out.
  • The common actions matter most. The player jumps hundreds of times and opens the credits once.
  • Juice can't fix a loop that doesn't work. It makes a good loop feel great. Fix the loop first.

Feedback ideas

Feedback ideas
Action Cheap, effective feedback
Jump, land A sound; a small squash of the sprite; dust
Collect A sound; a brief scale-up before disappearing; a number
Hit an enemy A flash; a short freeze; a knock back; a number
Get hurt A sound; the screen flashing red; the camera shaking
Win Music; something on screen that lasts a moment longer than expected

In class

  1. Play a bare prototype, then the same prototype with feedback. Ask which is the better game.
  2. Students list their game's three most common actions.
