Week 13 · in your own game · submitted at the end of class

Three classmates play your game while you watch. You don't help, explain or apologize. You take notes, and then you choose the three changes that matter most.

Before class

  • Bring a build that runs on a lab computer. Test it on a second computer first.
  • Bring your three playtest questions from Week 12.
  • Copy the notes table below into a document, once for each player.

What you'll learn

  • How to run a playtest without influencing it
  • How to record what players do
  • How to turn observations into a short, prioritized list of changes

Part 1 — Set up

  1. Start your game at its title screen.
  2. Have your notes ready, one table per player.

Part 2 — Run three sessions

For each player, about ten minutes:

  1. Say only: "Play this, and say what you're thinking."
  2. Then stay silent. No hints, no "that's a bug," no "it's supposed to…"
  3. Take notes as they play.
  4. If they're completely stuck for two minutes, note it, then show them past that point.
  5. At the end, thank them, then ask your three prepared questions. Write their answers down.

Then swap: you play two or three classmates' games for them.

Part 3 — Look for patterns

Put your three tables side by side.

  • What did two or three players do that you didn't expect?
  • Where did more than one player get stuck?
  • Which section took the most tries? Is that the difficulty you planned in your ten-pager?
  • What did players do that disagrees with what they said?
