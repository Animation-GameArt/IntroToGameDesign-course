In this tutorial the player from Tutorial 1 runs along a rail: a winding, climbing path drawn with a spline. The player can step a little to either side and jump, but can't fall off, because they always follow the rail.

Limiting movement to a path is what makes a 3D game 2.5D. The world and art are 3D, but the player moves along a line. With the camera behind, it plays like a narrow mountain path or an endless runner. With the camera at the side, it plays like a 2D platformer whose path curves through a 3D world, as in Klonoa, Pandemonium! or Kirby and the Forgotten Land's side sections.

Time: about one lab session.

Before you start

  • You've finished 2.5D Tutorial 1 — Third-Person Controller, and have the Player prefab and Follow Camera set up.
  • You're working in the 3D course project. It includes the Splines package.

Part 1 — A new scene

  1. Open Tutorial25D_Behind and File → Save As… Assets/_Game/Scenes/Tutorial25D_Rail.unity.
  2. Delete the blocks, ramp, coins and checkpoint. Keep the Ground, Player, Follow Camera, Fall Zone and HUD.
  3. Move Ground down to 0, -2, 0, so the rail can float above it in places.

Part 2 — Draw the rail

A spline is a smooth curve through points called knots. You place the knots; Unity draws the curve through them.

  1. GameObject → Spline → Draw Splines Tool. Name the new object Rail.
  2. In the Scene view, look down from above (click the Y arrow of the view gizmo, top right). Click to place a knot, then keep clicking to lay out a winding path about 60 units long, with 8 to 10 knots. Click and drag while placing a knot to make the curve around it smooth.
  3. Press Enter to finish.
  4. To change a knot afterwards, select Rail, choose the Spline tool context in the Scene view's tool overlay, click a knot, and move it with the Move tool.
  5. Make it climb: select two or three knots in the middle and raise them 2–4 units on Y. Keep slopes gentle; a steep climb looks odd when the player stays upright on it.

Make the path visible.

  1. Select Rail and Add Component → Spline Extrude. It builds a mesh along the spline.
  2. Set Shape to Road (if your version doesn't list Road, use Square). Set Radius to 0.75 and Segments Per Unit to 4. Give it the Blocks material.

The extruded mesh is centered on the spline. The player will stand on the spline line, so you'll set their height above it in Part 3.

How it works
The Rail Runner 3D doesn't use physics to move. Each frame it works out how far along the rail the player is (0 at the start, 1 at the end), moves that number forward or back, and places the player at that point, plus the sideways step and the jump height. That's why the player can't fall off.

Part 4 — Three ways to play it

Try each one. Keep the one that suits your game.

Behind the player (Forward And Back)

What you have now. Good for paths, chases and obstacle courses where the player looks ahead.

Side view: a 2.5D platformer (Left And Right)

  1. On Player, right-click → Create Empty. Name it Rail Facing. Leave it at 0, 0, 0.
  2. On the Rail Runner 3D: Controls Left And Right, tick Face Run Direction, Rail Facing Target Rail Facing, Rail Width 0.
  3. On Follow Camera: Tracking Target Rail Facing. Follow Offset 7, 2, 0 (7 units to the side). Rotation Damping (Y) 0.5.
  4. Press Play. Left and right run along the path, and the camera stays at the side as it winds. If right runs the wrong way, tick Swap Left And Right.

The Rail Facing child always faces along the rail, even when the player turns round, so the camera doesn't swing to the other side.

Endless runner (Auto Run)

  1. Controls Auto Run, Run Speed 9, Rail Width 3.
  2. Put the camera back behind the player (Tracking Target Player, Follow Offset 0, 3, -6).
  3. Select Rail and tick Closed on its Spline Container, so the rail loops back to its start.
  4. Press Play. The player runs by themselves; left and right dodge.

Part 5 — Obstacles and a finish line

The Rail Runner 3D ignores solid colliders, because it doesn't use physics to move. Anything the player should touch needs a trigger collider.

Something to jump over.

  1. A cube across the path, Scale 1.5, 0.5, 0.5, with its Box Collider set to Is Trigger, and Damage On Touch (Target Tag Player, Damage 1).
  2. The player needs Health and a Respawner (from Tutorial 1). Getting hit three times sends them back. After a respawn the Rail Runner snaps back onto the rail by itself.
  3. Add Checkpoints along the rail (trigger boxes with Checkpoint), so the player isn't sent all the way back.

Coins. Place Coin prefabs along the path, some at jump height.

A finish line. With the rail open (Closed unticked), the Rail Runner 3D's On Reached End runs when the player gets to the end. Add a Scene Changer → Load, or raise a Void Event Channel that shows a win screen, as in Exercise 06.

Check your work

  • The player starts on the rail, even if placed beside it in the scene.
  • Forward and back run along the curves and up and down the hills; the player stays upright.
  • Left and right step sideways, but never past the yellow edges.
  • Jumping works, with short hops on a quick tap.
  • The camera follows behind as the rail turns.
  • In the side view, the camera stays on one side, even when the player runs back.
  • Touching an obstacle costs health; running out sends the player to the checkpoint, back on the rail.
  • Reaching the end of an open rail runs On Reached End.

If something goes wrong

If something goes wrong
What you see What to do
"Rail is empty" in the Console Drag Rail into the Rail Runner 3D's Rail field
The player sinks into the path, or floats above it Change Height Above Rail
The player runs backward when pressing forward You drew the rail from the other end. Run the other way, or select the Spline and use Reverse Spline Flow from its context menu
The player speeds up and slows down along the rail Usually knots very close together. Spread them out, or delete extras
The camera flips to the other side in side view Use the Rail Facing child as the Tracking Target, and set Rail Facing Target
Obstacles don't hurt the player Their collider must be a trigger, and the player needs Health and the Player tag
The extruded path is lumpy at sharp corners Smooth the knots (click and drag their tangents), or raise Segments Per Unit
The player walks through walls next to the rail That's expected: the rail decides where the player can go. Keep scenery outside the rail's width

Ideas for your game

  • Branching paths: two rails, and a trigger at the fork that sets the Rail Runner's rail. (This needs a small custom script.)
  • A rail grind: a Tank Controller 3D for walking around, switching to a Rail Runner 3D when the player lands on a rail. (Also a custom script.)
  • A race: a closed loop, Auto Run, and a timer.
