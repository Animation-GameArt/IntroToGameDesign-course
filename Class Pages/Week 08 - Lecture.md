# Week 8 — Ex 07: Juice & Audio · Production backlog due

**Exercise:** Ex 07 — Juice & Audio. Feedback for three actions, music, and one camera shake, in the student's own game.

**Also due:** the production backlog, in Canvas by 11:59 PM the night before class: the ten-pager broken into tasks on a board, each with a week.

## Lecture topics, expanded

**Feedback and "juice."** A game should answer everything the player does. A jump gets a sound and a puff of dust. A hit gets a flash, a number and a knock back. Play a bare prototype, then the same prototype with feedback added, and ask the class which one is the better game. The rules are identical. Have students list their own game's three most common actions, because those are the ones worth polishing first: the player performs them hundreds of times.

**Camera shake, hit pause and restraint.** A short camera shake and a few frozen frames on impact make hits feel heavy. Show both in the course project. Then show what happens when everything shakes: nothing stands out, and some players feel ill. Feedback should be sized to the event. Small for a coin, large for a boss. That sizing is today's design decision.

**Sound effects, music and the mixer.** Sound does more for feel per hour of work than anything else. Cover the difference between sound effects and music, and why they're on separate volume controls. A sound that plays often should vary a little in pitch each time, or it becomes irritating. Point students to CC0 sound packs, and remind them that every pack goes in their credits file.

**Cutscenes and dialogue.** A Timeline plays a planned sequence: the camera moves, a character walks in, a door opens. Player controls are switched off while it runs. Dialogue uses Yarn Spinner, where a conversation is written as a plain script with choices. Be direct about scope. A solo game in eight weeks has room for an opening, an ending and a few lines in between. Most of a small game's story is told by what the level shows.
