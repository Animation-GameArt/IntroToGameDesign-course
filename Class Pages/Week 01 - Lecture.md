# Week 1 — Ex 00: Setup · Three game ideas due

**Exercise:** Ex 00 — Setup. Install Unity, make a practice project, tour the Editor and press Play.

**Also due:** three game ideas, posted to the Canvas Discussion. One line each. They're the raw material for the pitch in Week 3.

## Lecture topics, expanded

**What makes a game, and the core loop.** A game needs rules, a goal and feedback that tells the player how they're doing. Under those sits the **core loop**: the few actions the player repeats from moment to moment, such as run, jump, collect, or explore, fight, upgrade. Have students name the loop of a game they know in one line, then compare answers. A good loop is easy to say and still interesting on the hundredth repeat. This is the lens for every design decision this semester, and it's the first line of the one-sheet in Week 4.

**The genre map.** Genres are bundles of mechanics that players already understand. Walk the course's Genre Map: sixteen genres, mostly 2D, plus visual novels and two 3D options. For each, name the core loop and one well-known example. Then be direct about the course: students pitch a game that fits a genre on the map, because each one has a kit of scripts ready, and custom scripts are limited. A familiar genre isn't a lack of ambition. It leaves the student's time for the part of the game that is their own.

**How games get made.** Introduce the roles on a team (design, art, programming, audio, production, QA) and the difference between an indie team and a AAA studio. Then show the pipeline: concept and pitch, pre-production and design documents, production, alpha, beta, gold, post-mortem. The course follows the same path. Weeks 1–7 are concept and pre-production, weeks 8–15 are production, and week 16 is the post-mortem. Students work solo, so they hold every role at once, which is the best argument for a small scope.

**Unity Hub and the Editor.** The Hub manages Editor versions and projects. Everyone uses the same version, 6000.3.10f1, so the course project opens the same way on every machine. Tour the windows: Scene, Game, Hierarchy, Project, Inspector and Console. Show Play mode, and show that changes made during Play are lost when it stops. That single fact prevents a lot of lost work later.

## Connecting it forward

Nothing is built on today's practice project. It's thrown away. What carries forward is the vocabulary: core loop and genre are how students will describe their three ideas tonight, their pitch in Week 3 and their one-sheet in Week 4. Next week the real course project arrives, and every exercise from then on builds on the one before.
